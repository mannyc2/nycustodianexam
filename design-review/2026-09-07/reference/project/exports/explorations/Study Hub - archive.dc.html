Archive — study hub on the shared structure (turn 2)

Turn 1 stacked white cards on a light surface, which is why it read as a different site. This is the turn-1 structure applied to the dashboard: the dark header carries the one thing a returning learner came for — the resume — and the figures strip carries the numbers that used to be scattered across cards. Below it, section headers with a side note, hairline tables, four task cards and the dark footer. Only the header band and the two data sections read local state; everything else is identical for every visitor.

Calls I made here

  • Resume is the headline. The title names the session and the question; the white button resumes it and the outline button builds a different session. A new learner gets “Start with a set of 10” in the same slot. When the local read fails, a notice replaces the band and nothing else on the page changes.
  • The figures are the learner’s own. Due for review, answered this week, practice accuracy (labelled as not a score) and the pinned exam — the strip Home uses for site facts holds personal ones here.
  • Progress is a hairline table by official subject area. The three areas, numbered exactly as on Home, each with a bar or a count and a practice link; recent sessions sit beside it as a date list.
  • Your exam is a fact table, not a rail. Exam, test date, filing and pack, with the proof line and a Technical details disclosure holding the versions and exam number. The Not published chip is the only status mark on the page besides one Superseded item in review.

Same in both

  • The static overview is server-rendered. Everything a first-time visitor needs — what the exam is, a real question, coverage, sources — is in the HTML before any island mounts, and it stays on the page for returning learners rather than being swapped out.
