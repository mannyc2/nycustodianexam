Exams — which exam are you studying for

You preferred turn 1, so its structure became the library: a prominent PageHeader with a FigureStrip, SectionHeader, Timeline, StatusChip, SearchField and FilterGroup, RecordList with RecordDetail and Tabs, FactTable with proof and technical layers, NumberedSections, TaskCard and the prominent SiteFooter — each specified in the Component Library and appended to styles-additions.css. This page is turn 1 rebuilt from them, inside the two bans. Turn 3 is replaced rather than stacked.

Calls I made here

  • Turn 1’s bones, kept. Dark header with four figures, section headers with a side note, the four-card timeline and the no-filing notice, the master–detail exam board with its two tabs, the four task cards, the dark footer, and the same two frames. Only the words and the banned surfaces changed.
  • The bans, applied. Every uppercase tracked label is gone: dates are mono but sentence case, footer column labels are bold sentence case, and status chips are sentence case. Rows and the detail header put competition type, filing dates and status in separate slots instead of one agency–type–series–number string. Fact chips are sentence case and appear only where the state changes the reading; exam numbers, registry version, fact-sheet ids and routes sit under Technical details.
  • Still /exams/. The prominent header now carries this route’s task — which exam are you studying for — rather than Home’s proposition, which stays on Home.dc.html. The four figures answer the visitor’s first questions: how many exams, whether anything is open, how many subject areas, when the facts were checked.
  • Reading, not choosing. Picking a row shows its detail, and that is the only selection on the page: nothing is stored, no exam becomes “yours”, and practice is identical before and after. So there is no header chip, no “Your exam” marker and no disabled Selected — the detail’s two actions lead to what practice covers and to the next cycle. Archive — turn 1, the exams, then the tests you can take

A visitor's page. It answers where the exam cycle stands, what the announcements actually say, and what a test here looks like — before asking anyone to commit. Built on the real launch pack: two registry records, not a market of exams. The progressive study page you already have becomes /study; a returning learner gets a one-line resume strip here and lands on the dashboard from it.

Calls I made here

  • Everything here comes from the launch pack. Nassau open-competitive 60112026 and promotion 61012026 with their real filing dates, $50.00 fee, residency rules, jurisdiction amendments and seniority credit — plus the statewide entry-level plan, which has no announcement fact sheet at all.
  • The honest headline is that nothing is open. Both announcements closed in July and their 22 August exam date has passed with no post-administration notice found, so the page says the administration is unconfirmed rather than implying a result. A landing page has to survive the between-cycles months.
  • Subject areas belong to a record, not to the page. The board is a filterable list of announcement records with a detail beside it, and what is on the test — the areas, how each is asked, what we hold for it, and what is not published — is a tab of that detail. Adding a jurisdiction adds a row, not a page; a record that tests two of the three areas says two.
  • One page, two arrival states. The resume strip is the only difference between a first visit and a returning one — it appears when there is local progress to point at, and the ample and compact frames below show each case rather than one page labeled both ways.
