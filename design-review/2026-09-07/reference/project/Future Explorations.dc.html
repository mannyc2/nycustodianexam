Future explorations

Every control the prototype had that the implementation does not. Each one is drawn as it looked, then followed by why it is not in the release and what would have to be true first. Keeping them here means the route pages stay limited to working capability — no disabled teasers, no greyed-out switches, nothing that reads as coming soon.

How to read this page

  • The dashed frame means it does not exist. Inside each frame is a real drawing at the width it would ship at, so a future decision starts from a design rather than a description.
  • Two lines close every card. Why it is out, and the condition that would let it back in. If that condition is a piece of infrastructure, it says so, because the design is not the blocker.
  • Nothing here links into the route pages. The route DCs point here in one sentence and never render any of it.
Study lane

The 10-question quick set

The release leads with 45 questions, the smallest published set, and offers 60 and 90 beside it. Ten was the prototype's default and appeared in three places: the Study hub header, the first task card, and the Session Builder length control.

  • Future \u2014 a quick set as a fourth way to study
    bolt

    Quick set

    Ten questions, about five minutes, for a spare moment rather than a study session.

    Why it is out: no published set is ten questions long, so a ten-question result cannot be compared with anything and reads as a score against a test that does not exist. As a default it also taught the wrong scale \u2014 learners who practiced in tens were surprised by 45.

    Worth revisiting when: there is a reason to offer a deliberately unscored warm-up. It would need its own name, its own result screen that reports nothing as accuracy, and a place in the history list that cannot be mistaken for a practice set.

Offline lane

Controlling a download in progress

Both of these need a resumable transfer underneath them. Neither is worth drawing further until that exists, because the shape of the control depends on where the bytes are allowed to sit. See Offline for what shipped instead.

  • Future \u2014 Pause and Cancel under the progress bar

    Launch pack 2.1.0

    Downloading
    11.8 MB of 19.1 MB.

    Why it is out: Pause implies stored bytes and Cancel implies a transfer the page controls. The manager has neither. A Cancel that only stops reading the response would be a lie about what is happening to the network and the disk.

    To bring it back: ranged requests with a durable staging record \u2014 so a pause has somewhere to leave the bytes and a cancel has something to delete.

  • Future \u2014 a paused state that promises stored progress

    Launch pack 2.1.0

    \u25b2Paused

    The download stopped when the connection went away. What has already arrived is kept, and it will continue from there rather than starting again.

    Why it is out: nothing keeps the partial file. The sentence was the most specific promise on the page and the only one that was false. It is now the plain \u201cupdate did not finish\u201d card, which says the next attempt starts from the beginning.

    To bring it back: the same resumable transfer, plus a rule for how long a half-written copy may sit on the device before it is discarded \u2014 otherwise a paused download quietly holds 12 MB of quota forever.

Settings lane

The two preferences no code reads

Both looked reasonable and neither was asked for. See Settings for the two preferences that do exist.

  • Future \u2014 \u201cOpen on\u201d startup destination

    Where this site starts when you open it.

    Why it is out: nothing reads it. Every entry point is already a real URL a learner can bookmark, and the Study page leads with whatever is resumable. A preference that silently redirects the home page also breaks the back button in the way learners report as the site being stuck.

    Worth revisiting when: there is evidence people keep landing somewhere they did not want. The fix is more likely a better resume card on Home than a setting.

  • Future \u2014 \u201cPrefer text-equivalent items\u201d

    Why it is out: there is no per-question switch to honor it. Question items are text prompts already, and a hazard drill offers the picture route and the nonvisual route as two ways into the same scene, chosen there. A global preference would imply a substitution the content model does not make.

    Worth revisiting when: illustrated question items exist with reviewed text equivalents keyed to the same answer. Until then the honest control is the one on the drill itself.
