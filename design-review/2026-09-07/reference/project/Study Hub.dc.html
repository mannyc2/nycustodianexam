Practice and activity — before the first answer, and after nineteen

Two ends of the same page. A learner with nothing recorded gets a hub that starts them rather than reports on them: the header is the first action, what practice covers reads next, the ways to practice follow, and the two lists that would be empty stand as dashed EmptyStates that say what will fill them. A learner nineteen saved answers in gets the same page with its lists populated — items ready for review, saved activity by kind, and a per-attempt history that reads the same at four rows and at forty.

Calls I made here

  • The record is an attempt, not a session. The product saves one record per answered question and per scene response, plus one per finished review — there is no session object to resume or to score. So the history lists attempts (Question 12, Hazard scene 3, Review · question 9) with what was saved and a link back to the explanation, and the header offers a new set rather than a resume.
  • Review is a queue, not a schedule. Nothing is due on a date. Missed and flagged questions and hazard mistakes are ready until you finish them, so the section reads Ready for review with its two reason counts in their own labelled slots. There is no due-today or due-tomorrow figure, and nothing is queued for being corrected after an attempt.
  • Counts, never accuracy. Nothing computes a percentage, a weekly total or a per-area figure, so Where you stand is gone. Saved activity names the three things you can practice and states how much of each is saved; the subject areas keep their one authoritative home in What practice covers.
  • Start, not status. With nothing recorded the figures strip carries what is here to study — 90 questions, 18 scenes, 65 tool records, 3 areas — instead of four zeros, and the ways to practice sit directly under the header.
  • Empty is a standard. Ready for review and Recent activity render the EmptyState: dashed border, a heading that names what is absent, one sentence on what creates it, at most two actions. Unavailable is never dashed — the read-failed state (tweak) puts a warning notice in the same slots, and unreadable attempts sit in a solid-border list.
  • The page states its own scope. Nothing here selects an exam. The header names the series and the one question bank, What practice covers is the first section a first-time learner reads, and the closing section sends the comparison to the announcement itself instead of restating its dates.

What practice covers

One question bank, the three announced areas

Ninety questions written against the state test guide for the entry-level custodians and janitors series, and against the Nassau announcements that list the same three areas. Choosing 45, 60 or 90 changes how many you answer, never where they come from.

  • 1Cleaning tools and their uses. Recognizing a tool from a drawing or a description, and matching it to the job.
  • 2Tools for minor maintenance and repair. Hand tools for small repairs, and when a tool has become unsafe to use.
  • 3Health and safety in custodial work. Safe practices, chemicals, protective equipment, and what is wrong in a scene.

Where this comes from: New York State Department of Civil Service, Test Guide for the Entry-Level Custodians and Janitors Series, read August 25, 2026.

What it does not do

  • removeIt does not know which exam you are taking. Reading an exam page leaves your practice exactly as it is.
  • removeIt has no questions written for a single announcement, and no secure or recalled test material.
  • removeIt keeps counts of what you answered and reviewed, never a score and never a prediction of one.
