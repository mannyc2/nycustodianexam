# SCOPE.md
Scope-of-test extraction. Each line = concept/fact | source tier | source/citation | editorial boundary.

## Purpose
This file defines the lawful/source-backed knowledge universe for original practice content for the NYS Entry-Level Custodians and Janitors series. It does **not** contain secure or recalled exam items and does not claim official weighting.

## Official blueprint
### Cleaning Tools and Their Uses
Candidates identify cleaning tools/equipment from drawings and know their uses. The official DCS sample establishes recognition/use reasoning rather than brand/product trivia. [OFFICIAL — DCS Entry-Level Guide]

### Tools Used for Minor Maintenance and Repair
Candidates identify tools from drawings and know their appropriate uses in minor maintenance/repair. Official sample establishes elementary hand-tool recognition/use. [OFFICIAL — DCS Entry-Level Guide]

### Health and Safety Issues in Custodial Work
Candidates identify health/safety problems in workplace drawings and know appropriate safe practice. Official sample establishes basic PPE/hazard reasoning. [OFFICIAL — DCS Entry-Level Guide]

### Format boundary
Drawings are expressly not necessarily to scale. The guide is a scope/sample guide, not an item-count/weight disclosure. [OFFICIAL]

## Cleaning-tool knowledge
Practice content may test recognition, intended use, and obvious misuse of common custodial cleaning tools/equipment when supported by official occupational/institutional sources. Editorially useful families include:
- brooms/brushes/dusters;
- mops, buckets, wringers;
- floor-machine families;
- vacuums/extractors;
- squeegees/window-cleaning tools;
- restroom/fixture-cleaning tools;
- carts, dollies/hand trucks where occupationally supported;
- PPE used in cleaning tasks.

### Safe scope
- Ask what a tool is for, what distinguishes it, or which task it fits.
- Ask which obvious use is unsafe/ineffective when supported.
- Do not invent product-specific chemical ratios, pad colors, RPM thresholds, dwell times, or manufacturer-only procedures as universal exam rules.

## Cleaning procedures
Potential original rule/application content can cover source-backed general concepts such as:
- remove loose soil before wet cleaning where source supports;
- use appropriate caution/wet-floor controls;
- follow label/SDS and employer chemical procedures;
- avoid mixing incompatible chemicals;
- use PPE required for the task/product;
- maintain clean/usable tools and avoid cross-contamination where institutional guidance supports;
- use machine/tool only for intended surfaces/tasks and in accordance with training/instructions.

Product-specific details must remain scoped to the cited product/instruction, not generalized.

## Minor-maintenance / repair knowledge
Entry-level practice should remain at recognition and elementary use/safety level. Appropriate families, when sourced, include:
- hammers/mallets;
- screwdrivers;
- pliers;
- wrenches;
- saws;
- measuring/marking tools;
- levels/squares;
- fastening tools (including the official staple-gun sample concept);
- simple plumbing hand tools;
- ladders/basic access equipment at recognition/safe-use level;
- basic electrical hand-tool recognition only where safe and supported.

### Scope exclusions / caution
Do not drift into high-level building-operation content such as:
- boiler/HVAC diagnosis;
- refrigerant servicing;
- combustion analysis;
- supervisory planning/training;
- skilled electrical repair procedures;
- welding/brazing operation;
- powered equipment requiring specialized licensing/training unless the question is only broad recognition/safety and supported by entry-level occupational evidence.

## Health & safety knowledge
Original hazard-scene content may cover obvious custodial workplace hazards and safe responses supported by OSHA/NYS/institutional sources, including:
- slip/trip/fall hazards;
- wet floors and warning controls;
- blocked egress;
- unsafe ladder use;
- unsecured cords/hoses;
- improper lifting/material handling;
- PPE omissions where PPE is required;
- chemical labeling/storage/mixing hazards;
- electrical/water interaction;
- sharp/broken-object handling;
- unsafe stacking/storage;
- fire/combustible obstruction basics;
- sanitation/biohazard exposure precautions at general level;
- machine guarding/cord condition at broad recognition level.

### Hazard editorial rule
A scene can contain multiple hazards and decoys. The bank's environment/hazard distribution is editorial and must not be represented as official exam weighting.

## Chemical safety
Safe general facts include:
- follow manufacturer label and SDS/employer program;
- never assume two cleaners are compatible;
- incompatible chemical mixing can generate dangerous gases/reactions;
- use required PPE/ventilation and approved containers;
- keep labels legible and containers properly identified;
- do not use food/drink containers for chemicals.

Avoid turning internet folk rules into exam truths. Prefer OSHA, NIOSH, EPA, NYS, school/BOCES or manufacturer primary guidance.

## PPE
Practice may cover recognition/use of gloves, eye protection, hearing protection, and other task-appropriate PPE where supported. The official sample explicitly establishes gloves as protection against germs/chemicals in custodial context. Do not claim a specific glove material is universally correct without product/hazard-specific evidence.

## Ladders/access
General source-backed concepts suitable for safety practice:
- use the correct ladder type/height for task;
- inspect before use;
- maintain stable footing;
- do not stand on prohibited top steps/caps where ladder instructions/standards say not to;
- do not improvise unstable elevation;
- maintain appropriate contact and positioning;
- lock/spread stepladder components as designed.

Do not write detailed fall-protection or lift-operation questions beyond verified entry-level scope.

## Material handling
Potential sourced concepts:
- use carts/hand trucks/dollies for loads where appropriate;
- keep route clear;
- avoid overload/unstable stacking;
- use safe lifting posture/team assistance/mechanical aid consistent with institutional guidance;
- do not obstruct vision or exits.

## Floor machines
Recognition distinction between low-speed rotary floor machine and high-speed burnisher is editorially valuable and occupationally relevant. Questions should focus on visible configuration, intended broad task family, and safe recognition. Avoid unsupported universal RPM cutoffs/pad-color claims.

## Vacuums / carpet equipment
Differentiate broad equipment roles only where sourced:
- dry vacuum vs wet/dry vacuum;
- carpet extractor vs ordinary vacuum;
- ride-on/industrial variants at recognition level when occupationally supported.

Do not infer a machine's exact capability from appearance alone when designs vary.

## Plumbing hand tools
Entry-level recognition can include common tools such as pipe wrench, adjustable wrench, pliers, plungers, basic drain/fixture tools where sourced. Strong practice pairs include pipe-vs-adjustable wrench and cup-vs-flange plunger, but “confusable pair” is an editorial label, not official taxonomy.

## Carpentry/basic repair tools
Recognition/use may cover hammer types, saw types, screwdrivers, measuring/leveling tools, staple gun, clamps, planes, etc., provided evidence supports ordinary minor repair context. Keep advanced joinery/structural work out of scope.

## Electrical recognition
The official staple-gun sample includes a soldering gun as a distractor, showing basic recognition can include common electrical/soldering-tool forms. That does not establish electrical repair as an entry-level operational domain. Keep questions at identification/basic safe-use boundary unless stronger sources exist.

## Occupational evidence policy
O*NET “Tools Used” for Janitors and Cleaners can support occupational plausibility but does not itself prove exam inclusion or official weighting. Use it as corroborative scope evidence, not as a blueprint.

Direct occupational examples recovered include hand trucks/equipment dollies, safety glasses, ear plugs, steam-cleaning equipment, hedge trimmers, snow blowers, ride-on industrial vacuums, and various higher-risk equipment. Higher-risk entries require explicit editorial review before admission.

## High-level separation
The High-Level Custodians series covers written instructions, building cleaning, building operation/maintenance, HVAC operation/routine maintenance, and supervision/training. Do not import HVAC troubleshooting, boiler operation, supervisory scheduling/training, or similar high-level content into the entry bank merely because a higher title shares the word “custodian.”

## Source hierarchy for item writing
Preferred:
1. controlling DCS/Nassau announcement/test guide;
2. statute/regulation/official government safety guidance;
3. official school/BOCES/institutional facilities guidance;
4. recognized occupational standards/databases;
5. manufacturer technical guidance for narrowly scoped product/tool facts;
6. reputable secondary vocational sources.

Commercial test-prep material and candidate anecdotes do not establish correctness.

## Original-question construction rules
Every scored item should have:
- original wording and original/rights-cleared illustration if visual;
- one defensible correct answer;
- distractors that fail for specific source-backed reasons;
- explanation of correct choice;
- explanation for each distractor;
- source citation(s);
- domain/family/confusion tags;
- entry/high-level compatibility tag;
- security provenance confirming it was not derived from recalled/secure content.

## Visual question rules
- Use original line art; do not copy official sample drawings.
- Geometry must be coherent enough that the intended feature, not AI artifact, determines the answer.
- Multiple angles are useful for tool learning, but the test guide does not promise any fixed view.
- Images are not to scale unless scale is explicitly part of an original learning task.
- Before commitment, accessible labels/descriptions cannot reveal the answer; after commitment, full description is provided.

## Hazard-scene rules
- Use original school/custodial environments.
- Define zones and accepted hazard targets in metadata.
- Include plausible nonhazard decoys.
- Track missed hazards and false positives separately.
- Provide full post-attempt explanation and source basis.
- Never reconstruct a candidate-described official scene.

## Scoring/product truthfulness
Until official documentation resolves the unknowns:
- no “official-length” practice test;
- no official domain weighting;
- no predicted converted score;
- no passing probability;
- no assumption that 70 final rating = 70% raw correct;
- no assumption that all items are equally weighted/scored;
- no assumption about pretest items.

Selectable 45/60/90 sets and a two-hour endurance packet are explicitly site-designed rehearsal formats.

## Test-day/logistics facts suitable for FAQ
Where current source remains controlling:
- two-hour allowance for this series;
- multiple-choice written test;
- drawings may be used in all three areas and are not necessarily to scale;
- general state testing guidance: photo ID/admission notice, No. 2 pencils, prohibited notes/electronics, quiet handheld calculator only when permitted/not otherwise prohibited;
- most covered tests do not deduct for wrong answers, so answer every question unless instructions say otherwise;
- final rating/passing threshold is not necessarily raw percent correct.

Administration-specific location/time/medium must be drawn from current notice.

## Out-of-scope sources for correctness
Do not source answer keys from:
- Docsity/Stuvia/Studypool “verified answers” pages;
- Quizlet-style user sets without independent sourcing;
- Reddit/Facebook candidate recollections;
- commercial practice-book answer keys treated as official;
- AI-generated summaries without primary-source verification.

They may be studied only as competitive/UX landscape or vocabulary signals, not as exam-fact authority.
